# Perfect Competition (Garden) — Engagement Brief

## The problem
Making planting decisions for a farm with 64 beds. Crops are tomatoes, carrots, and mesclun, with limitations associated with maximum beds for each crop - 20 tomato, 20 carrot, 30 mesclun. The selection cannot be undone throughout the season. Tomatoes are $8,800 with 10% diminishing returns and the highest labor at 2.5hrs/wk/bed. Carrots are $2094/bed with 2.5% diminishing returns and the lowest labor of 0.833hrs/wk/bed. Mesclun is $2700/bed with 1.25% diminishing returns and 1.25hr/wk/bed. The staffing model includes one primary farmer and up to four temporary workers, who are less productive depending on the crop type. The problem is to decide how many beds of each make sense to plant based on the constraints.

## What I am assuming
- We can get adequate supply of seeds for all planting needs
- We will be able to find however many temporary workers we desire to hire

## Hypothesis
I expect that the mix will maximize tomatoes based on their price per bed with 18 beds, then 16 beds of carrots based on the lower fertilizer costs, even though they have the lowest price per bed, then have slightly less mesclun because of higher fertilizer costs with 14 beds. 

## How I would know I was wrong
If tomatoes are not the dominant crop, it would indicate that the diminishing returns and higher fertilizer cost have a greater impact than what I hypothesize.
