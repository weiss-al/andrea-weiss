# Perfect Competition (Garden) — Engagement Brief

## The problem
Making planting decisions for a farm with 64 beds. Crops are tomatoes, carrots, and mesclun, with limitations associated with maximum beds for each crop - 20 tomato, 20 carrot, 30 mesclun. None of the prices are available and the selection cannot be undone throughout the season. Tomatoes are about 4 times more expensive per bed than carrots or mesclun. The staffing model includes one primary farmer and up to four temporary workers, who are less productive depending on the crop type. The problem is to decide how many beds of each make sense to plant based on the constraints.

## What I am assuming
- We can get adequate supply of seeds for all planting needs
- We will be able to find however many temporary workers we desire to hire

## Hypothesis
I expect that the mix will maximize carrots with 18 beds, then have slightly less mesclun because of higher fertilizer costs with 14 beds. Tomatoes have the highest cost per bed and I expect that it will be the lowest with 6 beds.

## How I would know I was wrong
If the number of beds is different, particularly if a different crop is dominant.
