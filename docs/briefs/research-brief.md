# Assessment of Factors Underpinning Successful Pay-What-You-Want Businesses

## The topic
I want to evaluate the various factors of a business model that make a pay-what-you-want (PWYW) model successful.  In the PWYW structure, there are social factors prompting people to pay when the supplier is not requiring it. I want to evaluate what products/services are most conducive to this model and what factors of a business (location, community, mission, etc) make the model successful.


## What I am assuming
- The businesses undertaking this model are in-fact holding to a PWYW structure and not otherwise incentivizing/requiring people to pay for the product/service.
- The data on existing PWYW businesses can be replicated elsewhere and any profitability would be replicable over time. 

## Hypothesis
I expect that PWYW will be most feasible for goods/services that are typically more elastic, and those that inherently have diminishing marginal utility and low seller's marginal cost. I expect the necessity of inelastic goods would drive people to take the necessity for free - the perception that a product is a necessity would lower how obligated the buyer feels to pay. The diminishing marginal utility would limit the amount people want to take excessively, i.e. I don't need 12 cups of coffee, even if it is free. I also expect that a closer community or smaller town will be conducive to this model based on social pressure to support one another. 

## Method
Analysis of existing literature on PWYW models and the factors that underpin the business model. I expect that existing research will incorporate both successful and unsuccessful PWYW businesses.

## Model
Build a model to assess the feasibility of a PWYW model based on a businesses fixed costs, marginal costs for the product/service, marginal utility of the product for the buyer, and the obligation a buyer feels to pay based on community size/mission/other factors determined from the initial analysis.

## How I would know I was wrong
If the PWYW model held for a broader range of goods/services or in larger communities, it would indicate there are additional factors to consider on why someone would pay when they do not have to.
