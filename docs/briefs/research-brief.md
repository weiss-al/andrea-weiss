# <Engagement> — Assessment of Factors Underpinning Successful Pay-What-You-Want Businesses

## The topic
I want to evaluate the various factors of a business model that make a pay-what-you-want (PWYW) model successful. The PWYW model inherently goes against fundamental economic concepts that presume that a consumer will always pay the lowest possible cost for a good. In the PWYW structure, there are social factors prompting people to pay when the supplier is not requiring it. I want to evaluate what products/services are most conducive to this model and what factors of a business (location, community, mission, etc) make the model successful.


## What I am assuming
- The businesses undertaking this model are in-fact holding to a PWYW structure and not pressuring people or otherwise incentivizing/requiring people to pay for the product/service.
- The data on existing PWYW businesses can be replicated elsewhere and any profitability would be replicable over time. 

## Hypothesis
I expect that PWYW will be most feasible for elastic products that inherently have diminishing returns because the necessity of inelastic goods would drive people to take the necessity for free, and the diminishing returns would limit the amount people want to take more. I also expect that a closer community or smaller town will be conducive to this model based on social pressure to support one another. 

## How I would know I was wrong
Analysis of existing literature on PWYW models and the factors that underpin the business model. If the PWYW model held for a broader range of goods/services or in larger communities, it would indicate there are additional factors to consider on why someone would pay when they do not have to.
