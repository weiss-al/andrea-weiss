# <Engagement> — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Hire 3 temp workers for the season, plant 10 tomato, 19 carrot, 28 mesclun for an optimized profit of $16,586 where temp workers are modeled as whole humans rather than scaled by the hour.

## Why
For tomatoes, the marginal cost rises because of the higher rate of diminishing returns with each additional bed. The higher field hours also takes up a lot of the farmer's time. We see some drop in cost when the temporary workers are performing the labor, but again the diminishing returns limits tomatoes, as expected.

Carrots and mesclun are nearly maxed out, and only limited by labor cost for temporary workers. 

## The judgment call
The primary judgement call here is to model temporary workers as whole for their hire cost of $25,000 for the season. I made this choice based on realistic hiring and training constraints. I would not expect the farmer to be able to hire for a temporary worker to only add a few hours. 

## What would change my answer
This model could be adapted to reflect the ability to hire temporary workers for only a select few hours vice the total commitment of $25,000 for the season. Alternatively, if we kept workers as "lumpy", the model could also be adapted to have one of the 3 temporary workers work more than 1440 hours throughout the season (a few hours of overtime) which could push the number of beds up slightly based on an OT rate for those workers.
